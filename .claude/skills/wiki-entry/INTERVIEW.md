# Interview question bank

Draft every question's options from the source so the person mostly picks rather than writes. One topic per question.

## Always ask

Even when the source seems to answer these, they are judgement calls the person owns:

1. **Pitch**: "Which one-line pitch fits?" (offer 2–3 drafts).
2. **Visual metaphor**: "Which everyday picture should carry the explainer?" (offer 2–3, e.g. "a scale that shows odds", "a quiz with multiple-choice answers").
3. **Non-tech reader**: "Who is the explainer for?" (e.g. recruiters, leadership, clients, any colleague).
4. **Off the page**: "Is anything in the source sensitive and must stay out?" (client names, internal costs, people, unreleased plans).

## Ask when the source leaves it open

**Purpose and status**
- Why was this built: learning, a client need, a product bet?
- What state is it in, and is anyone using it?
- What happens next: keep going, park it, hand it to a team?

**Outcome**
- What result matters most to a reader: speed, cost, accuracy, a new capability?
- Which number best shows that result, and is it fine to publish?

**Explainer**
- Which worked example should the reader follow end to end?
- Should the explainer have an interactive figure? If it would call a paid API, is that approved, and at what cost per click?
- Where does a human stay in charge, in plain words?

**Tech**
- Which decisions were hardest, and what was the rejected alternative?
- What failed or surprised you?
- What should an engineer reusing this watch out for?
- Which links are safe to publish (repo, docs, reports)?

## Rules of the interview

- Up to 4 questions per round, most important first; recommended option first and labelled.
- When an answer opens a new gap, ask about it in the next round instead of guessing.
- Record each answer in the brief with `(asked: <date>)`.
